Nila is a privacy-first menstrual cycle tracking PWA for iPhone and iPad. Given
what period-tracking data has been used for, the design starts from a hard
constraint: the server must never be able to read a single health detail. Not a
period date, not a symptom, not a mood, not a note.

<NilaStill />

## Zero-knowledge by construction

The master key is derived from your password with PBKDF2 (600k iterations) and
never leaves the device. Every cycle and daily log is AES-256-GCM encrypted on
the client; the server stores only a random UUID and an encrypted blob, plus the
PBKDF2 salt and a wrapped master key. A 12-word BIP39 recovery phrase covers
account recovery without the password, and the same password unlocks the same
data across iPhone and iPad. "We don't look at your data" is a promise; *the
server structurally cannot* is an architecture, and Nila is the second.

## The tracking itself

There's a four-phase cycle model (period, follicular, ovulation, luteal) with
confidence-weighted prediction from your history, and a five-state period status
(none, approaching, late, active-started-today, active-ongoing) where each state
gets its own home-screen treatment. Predictions recalculate automatically
whenever cycles are added, edited, ended, or removed. Daily check-ins for mood,
energy, flow, symptoms and notes feed pattern-aware insight cards (*"you tend to
log cramps in your luteal phase"*), alongside a scrubable hormone graph and
phase-aware food and movement suggestions.

Built on Next.js 16 (App Router) and TypeScript, Tailwind v4 and Framer Motion,
Supabase for auth and encrypted storage only, and the `@noble` and `@scure`
libraries for the crypto. Installable, offline-capable, with a dark, light and
system theme that syncs across devices, and iOS push for period reminders.
