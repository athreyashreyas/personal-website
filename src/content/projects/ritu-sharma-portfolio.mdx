You land on a single painting, full bleed, with its title and medium and one
quiet invitation to see it up close. There's no hero copy or carousel competing
with the work; the newest piece simply fills the screen and changes as she adds
to the collection.

## Browsing, and buying

From there the Gallery is the heart of it: every original, in a masonry grid you
can narrow by medium, collection, price, and whether a piece is still available.
Tap any painting and you get its full page, the story behind it, and a way to
enquire or commission. It's built to make a small catalogue feel considered
rather than a wall of thumbnails.

## The real constraint

The hard part was never the front end. It was who maintains it. Ritu needed to
add and sell paintings, and edit her own About page, with no technical knowledge
and no phone call to me. So the CMS is a Sanity Studio embedded right in the site,
images go through Cloudinary, and there's a plain-language guide that walks her
through adding a painting from start to finish. If the site needs a developer to
update it, the brief has failed.

A few decisions followed from that. The whole interface runs out of the box on
placeholder content, so it could be built and reviewed before any backend was
wired up. SEO got real attention, with per-page metadata, dynamic OG images and
structured data, because a painter's site nobody can find isn't doing its one
job. And the escape hatches are written down: a runbook for moving off any
provider without re-uploading every image by hand, so she's never locked in.

Built on Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, Sanity v6, Cloudinary, and Tailwind,
with Resend for the inquiry emails.
