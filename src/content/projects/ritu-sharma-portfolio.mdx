A portfolio and sales website for a watercolour and oil painter. It has a
homepage with a latest-work gallery preview and an artist statement, a filterable
masonry gallery (`/artworks`) by medium, availability and price, full artwork
pages with the story behind each piece and an inquiry form, and About, Contact
(with commission requests) and Exhibitions pages.

<RituStill />

The real design constraint wasn't the front end. It was who maintains it. The
artist needed to add and sell paintings, and edit her own About page, with no
technical knowledge and no phone call to me. So the CMS is a Sanity Studio
embedded at `/studio`, with no separate deployment; images go through Cloudinary;
and there's a plain-language guide that walks her through adding a painting from
start to finish. If the site needs a developer to update it, the brief has
failed.

## The decisions worth noting

The site runs out of the box with empty placeholder content, so the whole
interface could be built and reviewed before any real backend was wired up.
Connecting Sanity and Cloudinary is what lights up the actual gallery and the
emails. SEO got real attention too, with per-page metadata, dynamic OG images,
`VisualArtwork` and `Person` JSON-LD, and a sitemap and robots file, because a
painter's site nobody can find isn't doing its one job. The escape hatches are
written down as well: a runbook for moving off any provider (Cloudinary
especially) without re-uploading every image by hand, and a staged
image-hardening playbook left deliberately switched off, so images stay full
quality until traffic actually justifies compressing them.

Built on Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, Sanity v6, Cloudinary, Tailwind, with
Resend for inquiry emails (falling back to server logs when it's not configured).
