Most habit trackers run on shame: a streak you can break, a plant that dies if
you skip a day, a score that ticks down. Harmony has none of that. It opens on
the Bloom, your whole week drawn as one flower with a petal for each area of your
life. Tend an area and its petal grows outward; leave it and the petal quietly
eases back. It reads a rolling two-week window, so one missed day barely moves
anything.

<HarmonyStill />

There's no number going down anywhere. A petal's length is just how tended that
area has been lately, weighted by how much it matters to you, so the flower leans
on its own toward the parts of life you said you cared about most.

## A day at a time

The daily view is the other half of it. Each habit is a round check with its
area's colour sitting underneath, and tapping it feeds the matching petal. The
habits you want *less* of live in their own "ease off" section as Tugs. You never
schedule those; you just note them on the days they happen, and they gently pull
an area back, so a week honestly shows both the lift and the drag.

## Under the hood

Harmony is a PWA: React and TypeScript on Vite, with Dexie (IndexedDB) as the
source of truth on each device and writes mirrored to Supabase, queued in a
durable offline outbox that flushes on reconnect. Push notifications come from a
separate Cloudflare Worker that signs its own VAPID payloads with the Web Crypto
API, no Node crypto anywhere.

The one decision I'd defend hardest is keeping the scheduling and drift engine in
a single shared package, used by both the app and the push worker. The rule that
decides whether a habit is due today has to be byte-identical in both places, or
reminders quietly fire on the wrong days. Duplicating it would have been faster
to write and a nightmare to trust.
