Most habit trackers run on shame: a streak you can break, a plant that dies if
you skip a day, a score that ticks down. Harmony has none of that. No streaks, no
shame, no plant pets, no score. The premise is the opposite. Your habits are how
you return to yourself, and one missed day should never read as failure.

<HarmonyStill />

## The Bloom

The centre of the app is a soft radial visual called the Bloom, your week drawn
as a living flower with one petal per area of life. A petal grows as you tend
that area and eases back when it goes untended. It reads a rolling two-week
window, recalculated daily, so a single missed day barely moves it.

Two things shape a petal. One is how much the area matters to you: lower-stakes
areas fill more readily, which pulls the eye toward the things you said matter
most. The other is how each habit inside it is doing, weighted the way you
choose. There are also Tugs, negative habits you want to ease off. They're never
scheduled; you just note them on the days they happen and they gently pull an
area back, so a week honestly shows both the lift and the drag.

## Under the hood

Harmony is a PWA: React and TypeScript on Vite, with Dexie (IndexedDB) as the
source of truth on each device. Writes mirror to Supabase and, when offline,
queue in a durable outbox that flushes on reconnect; live changes arrive over
Supabase Realtime with focus, online and poll backstops and an authoritative pull
on sign-in. Push notifications land within about a minute of the time you set,
delivered by a separate Cloudflare Worker that signs VAPID and `aes128gcm`
payloads with the Web Crypto API, with no Node crypto dependency.

The one decision I'd defend hardest is keeping the scheduling and drift engine
single-sourced in a shared package, re-exported as thin shims into both the web
app and the push worker. The rule that decides whether a habit is due today has
to be byte-identical in both places, or reminders quietly fire on the wrong days.
Duplicating it would have been faster to write and a nightmare to trust.
