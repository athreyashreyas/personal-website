Attend keeps every class above its attendance line, without the spreadsheet. Add
your classes, mark each session with a tap, and it tells you where you actually
stand: how many you can still miss, how many you'd need in a row to recover, and
where you'll land by the end of term.

<AttendStill />

The idea that shaped everything is that the class is the unit, not the semester.
A degree subject can sit under a semester, but a dance class or a hobby can stand
on its own. That one decision is why the same app works for a university
timetable and a twice-a-week pottery class, and it's the thing most attendance
apps get wrong by assuming everyone's life is neatly divided into terms.

## Looking forward, not just back

The number that matters isn't your current percentage. It's the projection.
Attend works out the classes still to come and the best and worst you can finish
on, then turns that into plain guidance: you can miss 3 more, or attend 4 in a
row to recover. You can also drop a class onto a future day as "scheduled" and
decide later how it actually went; planned classes never touch your percentage
until you mark them. When a holiday or exam week comes up, you can cancel a date
range across every class at once, and cancelled sessions drop out of the maths
automatically.

## Local-first, native-feeling

Reads come straight from Dexie (IndexedDB), so the app is instant and fully
usable with no signal; edits sync to your own Supabase account in the background.
It's built with Vite, React and TypeScript (strict), with Tailwind and Framer
Motion for the feel, a sidebar rail on iPad and a bottom tab bar on phones, all
safe-area aware. It installs to the home screen and works offline by default. The
whole point is that you can mark a class as you walk out of the room, on the
subway, with no connection.
