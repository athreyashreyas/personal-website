You open Attend to answer one question: am I still fine to skip tomorrow? The
dashboard answers it per class. Each card gives you the percentage as a ring and,
next to it, a plain-language badge that has already done the projection for you:
*can miss 3 of 8 left*, *attend all 4 left*, or a red *below 75%* when you've
slipped under the line.

<AttendStill />

That badge is the whole point. Your current percentage is history; what actually
matters is the number you'll finish the term on. Attend counts the sessions still
to come, works out the best and worst you can land on, and says it in words, so
you're not doing arithmetic in your head at the back of a lecture hall.

## Marking, in the corridor

Marking is meant to happen the moment a class ends, so it gets its own flow. Tap
the "mark today" banner and Attend hands you one class at a time: the name, the
time, and two big buttons. Present or Absent, then the next one slides in. If a
class got cancelled you say so and it drops out of the maths instead of counting
against you.

## The class is the unit

The decision that shaped everything is that the class is the unit, not the
semester. A degree subject can sit under a semester, but a dance class or a hobby
stands on its own, which is why the same app fits a university timetable and a
twice-a-week pottery class. Most attendance apps get this wrong by assuming
everyone's life divides neatly into terms.

Under the hood it's local-first: reads come straight from Dexie (IndexedDB), so
it opens instantly and works with no signal, while edits sync to your own
Supabase account in the background. Vite, React and strict TypeScript, with a
sidebar rail on iPad and a bottom tab bar on phones. It installs to the home
screen and works offline by default, because the whole point is marking a class
as you walk out of the room.
