Attend keeps every class above its attendance line without the spreadsheet. Add
your classes, mark each session in a tap, and it tells you where you actually
stand: how many you can still miss, how many you'd need in a row to recover, and
where you'll land by the end of term.

The design choice that shaped everything: **the class is the unit, not the
semester.** A degree subject can live under a semester; a dance class or hobby
can stand completely alone. That one decision is why the same app works for a
university timetable and a twice-a-week pottery class, and it's the thing most
attendance apps get wrong by assuming everyone's life is neatly termed.

## Looking forward, not just back

The number that matters isn't your current percentage — it's the projection.
Attend works out the classes still to come, the best-case and worst-case final
standing, and turns that into plain guidance: *you can miss 3 more*, or *attend 4
in a row to recover*. You can also drop a class onto a future day as "scheduled"
and decide how it actually went later; planned classes never touch your
percentage until you mark them. Breaks (a holiday or exam week) can be cancelled
across a date range for every class at once, and cancelled sessions drop out of
the maths automatically.

## Local-first, native-feeling

Reads come straight from **Dexie (IndexedDB)**, so the app is instant and fully
usable with no signal; edits sync to your own Supabase account in the background.
Vite + React + TypeScript (strict), Tailwind and Framer Motion for the feel, a
sidebar rail on iPad and a bottom tab bar on phones, all safe-area aware. It
installs to the home screen and works offline by default — the whole point is
that you can mark a class as you walk out of the room, on the subway, with no
connection.
